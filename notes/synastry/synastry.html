Synastry Chords

Two birth charts as one chord. Every aspect between one chart's planets and the other's sounds its interval, each planet in the voice of its sign; with headphones, the first person sits in your left ear and the second in your right. Switch to Transits to let today's sky play over the two of them.

Between the two charts

    Tap a chord to bring it forward. Orbs are William Lilly's moieties; the intervals are Kepler's: sextile a minor third, square a fourth, trine a fifth, opposition an octave.

    Example: Johannes Kepler (6 January 1572, 2:30 pm local mean time, Weil der Stadt, after his own horoscope) and William Lilly (11 May 1602, Diseworth, time unknown), both on the Gregorian calendar. Enter your own charts below; they stay in this browser.

    How it sounds

    Each planet has a register by speed, fast planets high and slow ones deep, as Kepler had it. An aspect sounds its interval above the slower planet's A: A3 when an outer planet is involved, A4 otherwise. Both planets play that note together, each in its own sign. The element sets the tone (fire bright, earth hollow, air a flute, water nearly pure) and the modality sets how it moves (cardinal pulses, fixed swells, mutable wavers). In squares and oppositions the second voice sits a few hertz sharp, so they shimmer, quick at the edge of orb and slowing as they tighten. A low A holds underneath.
